FIELD NOTES / 01 · ROBOTS & SIMULATION BASICS

What makes up a robot
in a simulation?

Seeing a robot on screen is just the beginning. Making it move also takes a model, control and a world that follows physical rules.

01

Models: more than an outer shell.

A robot model describes how its body connects, along with mass, collision shapes and joints. Its visible appearance can differ from the shapes used to calculate collisions.

Looking like a robot on screen does not mean the model already has the corresponding movement abilities.

02

Joints: how the body can move.

Think of a robot arm as a chain of connected parts. Joints define whether adjacent parts can rotate or slide, and how far they can move. A joint in a model file usually describes this connection.

Understanding the joints tells you more about what a robot can do than its name alone.

03

Control: from a goal to actual movement.

A target position is not an action by itself. A controller repeatedly reads the current state and adjusts inputs to move the robot toward its goal. The model provides the body; control makes it act.

The same body can behave differently under different controllers.

04

Simulation: change, one step at a time.

A simulation updates positions, velocities and contacts step by step. In MuJoCo, mj_step is a common entry point for advancing one simulation step. Many small steps form the continuous motion we see.

When watching a demo, ask whether movement comes from contact and control, or from a program directly setting positions. Both have uses, but they should be described separately.

These are introductory concepts for understanding robots and simulation. Each demo describes its specific abilities and limitations.
